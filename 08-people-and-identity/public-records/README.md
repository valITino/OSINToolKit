# Public records

Official registries turn a name or company into hard facts: directors, addresses,
filings, judgments. Coverage and access rules vary enormously by country, and
privacy law (GDPR in particular) limits what you can see and what you can then do
with it.

## Where to look

- **Company registries.** Germany's [Handelsregister](https://www.handelsregister.de/)
  and [Insolvenzbekanntmachungen](https://www.insolvenzbekanntmachungen.de/) (insolvency
  notices); the UK's Companies House; OpenCorporates as a cross-jurisdiction
  aggregator. These give officers, addresses, and filing history.
- **Court records.** In the US, [PACER](https://pacer.uscourts.gov/) for federal
  cases; CourtListener/RECAP as a free front for much of it. Many jurisdictions
  publish judgments and case dockets online.
- **Property, licensing, and sanctions.** Land registries, professional-licence
  boards, and sanctions/PEP lists (OFAC, EU consolidated list) each turn a name into
  a record.

## Watch for

- **GDPR redaction.** EU member states restrict access to and reuse of personal
  data in public records; some registries require a stated purpose or charge per
  document. Absence of data is often law, not a dead end.
- **Jurisdiction.** Match the registry to where the person or company actually is.
  A clean UK search says nothing about a German entity.
- **Reuse limits.** "Publicly accessible" is not "free to aggregate and store."
  Have a lawful basis before you build a dossier. See [../../LEGAL.md](../../LEGAL.md).
