# Forums and wikis

Edit histories are a gift. Anywhere content is versioned - wikis, some forums,
issue trackers - the revision trail records who changed what, when, and sometimes
from which IP. That is often more revealing than the current text.

## Wikipedia and wikis

- Every article has a **View history** tab: each revision, its author (username or
  raw IP for anonymous edits), timestamp, and edit summary.
- Anonymous edits expose the editor's **IP address** - geolocate it
  ([02-network-and-ip](../../02-network-and-ip/README.md)) and check it against known
  ranges. Organisations editing their own pages from office IPs is a classic finding.
- Deleted or reverted content persists in history; a fact someone tried to remove
  is still there.

## Forums

- Registration dates, post counts, and signatures build a timeline and a persona.
- Quoted posts preserve content the original author later deleted.
- Cross-reference the username across platforms
  ([username-enum](../../08-people-and-identity/username-enum/README.md)) and search
  for it in paste dumps ([paste-sites](../paste-sites/README.md)).

## Method, not tooling

There is no durable single tool here - scrapers break and sites vary. Work from the
platform's own history/search features, archive aggressively
([Wayback](../../06-history-and-archives/web-archive/wayback-cdx-api.md),
archive.today) because threads get deleted, and record provenance as you go.
